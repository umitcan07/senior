Dear Senior Project Students,

Congratulations. You are embarking on your senior projects, the **capstone** experience of your undergraduate program. This is your opportunity to demonstrate the breadth and depth of what you've learned, working on a project you care about with a faculty advisor whose work aligns with your interests.

Senior Projects are different from earlier coursework in important ways. Unlike the larger team projects in software engineering courses, you'll be working individually or in small teams on a project you've selected with your supervisor. This is your chance to showcase your capabilities to future employers, graduate programs, or collaborators.

Senior Projects are highly visible. Faculty, industry partners, and graduate schools look at these projects as evidence of what you can do independently. A well-executed Senior Project becomes part of your professional portfolio, something you'll reference in interviews, applications, and conversations about your work for years to come.

This semester, all Senior Projects are required to maintain a Git-based repository (GitHub, GitLab, etc.) to organize and document your work. This isn't just a department requirement. It's a professional skill that directly benefits you.

Here's why this matters:
------------------------

First, prospective employers and graduate programs want to see not just what you built, but how you built it. A well-maintained repository demonstrates critical professional competencies: planning, organization, documentation, version control, and project management. These are fundamental skills in computing, whether you're heading to industry or research.

Second, repositories provide you with a concrete artifact to share. When interviewing or networking, you can point someone to your repository and say "here's what I worked on, here's how I organized it, here's my contribution." This is far more compelling than describing a project verbally or showing only a final report.

Third, repositories accommodate all types of projects. Whether you're writing extensive code, conducting theoretical research, performing algorithm analysis, or doing concept-driven work, a repository provides appropriate structure. For theoretical projects, think of your repository as a research notebook: tracking ideas, organizing documentation, managing LaTeX files, maintaining bibliographies, and recording decisions and progress.

Finally, this creates consistency and professionalism across our department's Senior Projects. These repositories become part of our departmental history, demonstrating the creativity, technical skill, and intellectual curiosity of our students. When we can point to a collection of well-documented, well-organized projects, it reflects positively on all of you and strengthens our program's reputation.

What This Means Practically
---------------------------

Your repository is yours to manage. How you structure it, what you track, and how frequently you update it should align with your project needs and your advisor's expectations. Think of your repository as one of the ways you communicate with your advisor and demonstrate progress, similar to status updates or milestone reports, but persistent and organized.

Different advisors may have different preferences for how they want to interact with your repository. Some may check it regularly; others may primarily rely on your meetings and presentations. This is normal professional practice: understanding your stakeholder's preferences and adapting your communication accordingly.

Your earlier coursework has prepared you for this. You've used version control and repositories before. The difference now is that this repository represents your work, your professional capabilities, and your ability to manage a substantial project from start to finish.

If you have questions about repository expectations or how to structure your work, start by discussing with your advisor. I'm also available to provide feedback on effective use of repositories if that would be helpful.

Looking Ahead

Senior Projects are challenging, but they're also rewarding. This is where you get to work on something meaningful with expert guidance. The repository requirement is designed to support you in this process: keeping your work organized, your progress visible, and your final product professional.

By the end of this experience, you'll have both a completed project and a well-documented record of how you got there. Both matter. Both will serve you well as you move forward in your career.

You'll be receiving further information from me soon regarding project deliverables and timelines. I should note that in addition to your deliverables, you are expected to present your work in person in a poster session. I am sure you must be aware of the poster sessions at the end of every semester.

We're genuinely excited to see what you create and hope you have a productive, satisfying, and enjoyable experience working on your projects.
